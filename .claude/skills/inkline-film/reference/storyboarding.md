# Storyboarding: think like the director, not the editor

A storyboard is not a list of claims with a scene each; that is a shot list, and it makes a montage. A storyboard
is a **story told in pictures**: someone or something changes, the viewer feels it, and the brand is the reason.
Do all of this in writing, and get the user's approval, before building a single scene. Animation is the easy part;
this is the part that decides whether the film is any good.

The style is fixed (`style-bible.md`). Everything below is invented for each script.

---

## 1. Brief → logline

Pull from the script:
- **who it's for**;
- **the one thing they must remember**;
- **what they should do after**: visit, download, buy, believe;
- **the tone**: warm, bold, calm, playful, premium.

Then write a **logline**: *"A ___ (protagonist) ___ (wants/does) until ___ (turn), and ___ (resolution = the
brand's moment)."*
- Weak: "Velmora coffee is roasted in small batches and opens a café."
- Strong: "A man on his way to work and a coffee bean on its way from the hills cross a city at dawn, and meet in
  one cup at Velmora's new café."

## 2. The concept device: the one idea the whole film hangs on

Pick one. It decides every shot. Never skip this; without it, you get a montage.

| Device | How it works | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Parallel journeys** | intercut two travellers (a person and a product) until they meet | launches, places, anything "made for you" |
| **One continuous take** | the camera never cuts; every scene is a push through the last (a match or a dive) | process, "behind the scenes", tech flows |
| **Object's point of view** | the film follows the product: a bean, a card, a parcel | craft, logistics, fintech |
| **Before / after** | the same frame twice, the world changed by the brand | apps, services, renovation |
| **Countdown / ritual** | a sequence of steps the audience knows, made beautiful | coffee, skincare, launches, sport |
| **Scale shift** | from the micro (a seed) to the macro (a city), or the reverse | growth, platforms, impact |
| **Reveal** | hide what we're looking at until the last shot ("it was a cup all along") | premium products, teasers |
| **Rhyme** | a recurring motif (a circle, steam, a door) returns in each scene and pays off at the end | any film; combine it with another device |

**Always choose a motif** too: one shape or motion that recurs in every scene and pays off at the end. For
coffee: circles (cherry, drum, tray, cup), or steam.

## 3. Protagonist and arc

- **Someone to follow.** A person (the `Person` figure: their walk, their hands, a hat, a coat colour that never
  changes) or a hero object (a bean, a parcel). One protagonist, recognisable in every shot they're in.
- **An emotional arc in three beats:**

| Beat | Feeling | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Set-up | the ordinary, a want | a dark city morning, a man setting out, half asleep |
| Turn | the change | the café's door opens; warm light; the smell (steam) |
| Payoff | the feeling the brand gives | the first sip; the street wakes up; the sign lights |

## 4. Beat sheet (30–40 s)

| Time | Beat | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 s | **Hook** | an image that makes you look: unusual, close, moving. Never a logo or a title card |
| 2–8 s | **Set-up** | who and where; the world's texture; the motif first appears |
| 8–24 s | **Journey** | 4–6 shots that build; intercut, escalate the pace, shorten the shots |
| 24–30 s | **Turn and payoff** | the meeting, the reveal, the emotional peak; the slowest, most beautiful shot |
| 30–35 s | **End card** | the logo, one line, and the action (date, place, URL) |

**The pace curve.** Shots get shorter towards the turn (4 s → 3 s → 2.5 s), then one long payoff (5–6 s). Draw
the curve in the table.

## 5. Shot grammar (write it for every shot)

| Column | Options |
|---|---|
| **Size** | EWS (establishing), WS, MS, CU, ECU (macro), OTS (over the shoulder), POV, TOP (overhead) |
| **Angle** | eye level, low (heroic), high (small, overview), top-down |
| **Move** | push in, pull out, dolly left/right, crane up/down, orbit, track (follows the subject), static + subject move |
| **Action** | what moves in frame: one clear action per shot |
| **Screen direction** | the protagonist always travels the same way (left → right = forward) until the turn |
| **Light** | dawn, day, dusk, night; it should change across the film to mark time passing |
| **Sound** | the foley the picture implies (footsteps, pour, door bell, grinder) plus the music's state |
| **In** | the join into this shot, and why |

**Rules:**
- **Vary the sizes.** Never three wides or three close-ups in a row. A good rhythm is EWS → MS → CU → ECU → WS.
- **One action per shot.** If you need "and", it's two shots.
- **Match cuts carry meaning.** Join the drum turning ↔ a bicycle wheel; the steam ↔ the morning mist; the cup
  from above ↔ the café's round table. Design both ends together, at the same place on screen.
- **Intercut with rhythm.** For parallel journeys, alternate A / B / A / B, and shorten each pair as they
  converge.
- **Keep words few.** 3–5 lines of on-screen text in the whole film. Let the pictures tell it. Headlines
  are for what pictures can't say: the name, a date, the one claim.

## 6. The world of the script (your props)

List 15–25 specific, filmable things only this subject has: what a specialist would recognise, not stock icons.
Mark the ones that move in a satisfying way (split, pour, turn, unfold, open, grow). These become the shots.

## 7. Sound plan

- **Music:** the genre that fits the tone (lo-fi jazz for a café; cinematic pulse for space; bright pop for an
  app); where it builds, where it drops out (before the turn), where it lands (the payoff).
- **Foley that makes it real:** footsteps synced to the walk, a door bell, the pour, the grinder's crunch, the
  cup on the saucer.
- **One silence:** a beat of near-silence just before the payoff makes it land.

## 8. End card

The logo (the brand's own file), one line, and the action: "Opens 1 November · 12 Market Street", "Download now",
"Book a table". The action must be real; in a sample, label it as fictional.

## 9. The board you show the user

1. **The logline, the device, the motif, the protagonist and the arc**, five lines in all.
2. **The pace curve:** shot durations, with the turn marked.
3. **A visual board**, one panel per shot. Each panel has:
   - a thumbnail sketch of the framing (horizon, subject, move arrow);
   - the shot size, angle and move;
   - the action;
   - the on-screen words;
   - the sound;
   - the join in.

   Publish it as a page (see the worked example `docs/boards/velmora.html`).
4. **The film's world list**, marking which subjects will be built and how (lathe / extrude / tube / leaf / blob
   / Person / 2D).

Wait for approval. Then build `src/films/<slug>/`, one scene per shot, and write the plan.

## 10. Check the board before you show it

- **Mute it and hide the words.** Can you still tell the story from the panels alone?
- **Is there a protagonist** we follow and care about by second 5?
- **Is the hook in the first 2 seconds** something you have not seen in a hundred ads?
- **Does every join mean something**, or is it just a cut?
- **Is there a payoff shot** that is slower and more beautiful than everything before it?
- **Could this board be another brand's film by changing the logo?** If yes, it is not specific enough: go back to
  §6.
